How Osmosis judges recall

Checkpoints vs. live stats

Live statistics summarize the full activity record and current question-level learning counters. A progress checkpoint is an automatic, compact roll-up saved after each quiz: one row per chapter/topic with exposure, mastery, average strength, missed answers, and growth since the previous checkpoint. It does not copy question text or duplicate the bank. This history makes topic-level growth and learning pace comparable over time.

Mastery and what changes it

A question becomes mastered the first time you answer it correctly. Earlier misses do not count against mastery. After it is learned, a miss removes its mastered mark; a later correct answer can restore it. Repeated misses while it is already unmastered do not keep dragging it down. Mastery percentages count questions currently marked mastered, not a lifetime average.

Practice priority and time

A separate strength estimate helps order review and fades gradually with time since the last attempt; it does not determine the mastered label. Answer speed is not used to score mastery or strength because questions have different reading demands. In timed runs, the clock pauses after an answer while you read the explanation, then resumes when you continue.

Clock and saved data

Answers and settings are saved in this browser. Study-time estimates use your device clock, so changing it can affect elapsed-time calculations. Paused breaks are excluded from quiz time. Export your data for a backup.

How the next run is picked

Runs prioritize unmastered questions first, weakest strength first; once those are covered, the weakest mastered questions fill remaining slots. Questions with a history of struggle stay dimmer and recur sooner, even after mastery. One parent idea appears only once per run, with at most one variant.
